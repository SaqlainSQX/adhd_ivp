# S: course submission evidence synthesis

Registered after R's primary simulation readout, before producing the synthesis.
This is reporting and traceability work, not another statistical experiment.
Keep the original submission and all immutable stage manifests untouched.

Create a separate course results guide through R and a machine-readable claim
map. Use only saved, validated aggregate evidence. No new predictions, fitted
models, query draws, noise settings, optimized weights or unseen-test claim.

The guide must distinguish:

- The original locked main temporal/static result, including no temporal win.
- D's paired same-query ChildPlay half-weight gain and E's source heterogeneity.
- F's small width improvement and G's block/temporal losses at their proper costs.
- L's UCO half-weight reversal and full-K4 versus uniform gain, preserving every
  budget and clearly identifying shared pretraining/eligible-pair limitations.
- M's analytical variance explanation, explicitly post hoc and not a new weight.
- Q/R's synthetic observation-error sensitivity, with no measured human rates,
  no clean-target guarantee for naive binary intervals and no clinical claim.
- Reproduction/delivery paths: N's aggregate demo, O/P's source-only verified
  aggregate builder, and the limitations of those checks. Distinguish replay
  reproducibility from rerunning inference or collecting new annotation evidence.

For every headline numeric statement, save the exact accepted source file,
JSON/CSV selector, metric, units and expected value in the claim map. Independently
resolve all selectors and compare values to the guide's machine-readable summary;
check all cited local paths exist, verify frozen main result/lock and prior
evidence manifests, and visually review any new overview figure. Preserve a
failed check as evidence and repair additively. No human-label files or raw data
in the guide/export. Do not silently replace historical results assessments.

Deliver a concise defensible project title, one paragraph contribution, proposed
report structure, method pipeline, results/limitations, demonstration commands
and likely viva questions. A course project need not claim a new theorem or
uniform benchmark gain. Explicitly identify any information that cannot be
recovered from accepted evidence rather than estimating or inventing it.

No random seeds or new label budgets are needed because S creates no scientific
outcomes. It should improve the quality and reviewability of the course package.
The standing continuation stays active; subsequent empirical work requires a
distinct useful registered question rather than further test-set parameter search.
